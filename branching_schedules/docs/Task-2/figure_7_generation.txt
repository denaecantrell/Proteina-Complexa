Figure 7 reproduction for test-time scaling of inference-time optimization

What Figure 7 shows
-------------------

Table 2 asks whether the pretrained generator is any good on its own. Figure 7
asks what happens when you spend extra compute at inference time searching for
better designs, and which search algorithm spends it best.

The x-axis is test-time GPU hours, not sample count. This is the key difference
from Table 2, which fixes 200 samples per target and reports one number. Figure
7 fixes a time budget instead and plots a curve, because the algorithms consume
compute at very different rates: one beam search step evaluates sixteen
candidates, while best-of-n just draws another sample.

The y-axis is average unique successes, meaning designs passing all three
criteria and then clustered with Foldseek, so 200 copies of one good binder
count once.

Two panels, 12 easy targets and 7 hard targets, and they do not agree:

    easy targets    Best-of-N ~137, Beam Search ~104, FK Steering ~97,
                    MCTS ~80, at 16 GPU hours

    hard targets    Beam Search ~26, FK Steering ~25, MCTS ~19,
                    Best-of-N ~18, at 32 GPU hours

That inversion is the most interesting thing in the figure. On easy targets,
drawing more independent samples beats branching. On hard targets, branching
beats drawing more samples. Best-of-N goes from first place to last.

This matters directly for Task 3. If branching only pays off on hard targets,
then testing branching schedules on easy targets means tuning a strategy that is
already losing there, and any difference between schedules would be noise around
a method that should not be used in that regime.


What the paper does
-------------------

    19 targets, 12 easy and 7 hard
    budget caps of 16 GPU hours per easy target, 32 per hard target
    five algorithms, plus the hallucination baselines BindCraft and BoltzDesign
    Best-of-N scaled up to 51,200 samples
    beam search and FK steering repeat the entire search until the clock expires
    GPU hours include generation AND evaluation time
    reward is interface pAE alone
    N=4, L=4, K=100 over 400 denoising steps; FKS inverse temperature beta=10

Measured on an A100.


What we are doing differently, and why
--------------------------------------

The difference is entirely compute. The paper's protocol is roughly

    16 GPU-h x 12 easy  +  32 GPU-h x 7 hard  =  416 GPU-hours per algorithm

and with five algorithms that is around 2,000 GPU-hours. On two shared cards
that is several months of wall-clock. It is not reachable, and no amount of
careful configuration makes it reachable.

So the experiment is restructured around a question that is answerable at small
scale rather than a figure that is not. Three cuts:

    targets      2 instead of 19, one easy and one hard, chosen so the
                 easy/hard inversion can still be observed
    algorithms   3 instead of 5 plus baselines: best-of-n, beam-search,
                 fk-steering. MCTS and the hallucination baselines are dropped
                 because they are not what this project is about
    budget       4 GPU-hours per (target, algorithm) instead of 16 or 32

That is 2 x 3 x 4 = 24 GPU-hours, about 12 hours of wall-clock on two cards.

Everything else is left at the paper's values. N=4, L=4, step_checkpoints at
100-step spacing over 400 denoising steps, and FKS temperature 0.1 are already
the repository defaults and already match Appendix H.2, so the stock config is
the paper's condition and needs no changes.

What this costs us: the absolute numbers will be far below the published curves,
because we stop at 4 GPU-hours where the paper runs to 16 or 32, and because an
RTX 6000 Ada hour buys roughly 0.77 of an A100 hour on this workload. The
y-values are not comparable to the figure.

What survives: the ORDERING of the algorithms at matched budget, and whether
that ordering inverts between the easy and the hard target. Ordering is a
within-experiment comparison, so it does not depend on the hardware or on
reaching the paper's budgets. That is the claim worth testing.


What the experiment tests
-------------------------

    easy target   does best-of-n beat beam-search and fk-steering
    hard target   does beam-search beat best-of-n

If both hold, Figure 7's qualitative structure reproduces at 1/80th of the
compute, and Task 3 should use hard targets.

If the easy result holds and the hard one does not, that is a statement about
scale: 4 GPU-hours is not enough for branching to pay off, and the crossover
needs a larger budget to appear.


Seeds
-----

The pipeline config pins seed: 5. Repeating a run without overriding it
regenerates byte-identical structures, so a budget sweep accumulates duplicates
and the unique-success curve goes flat while the raw count climbs. That flatness
looks like genuine diversity saturation and is not.

Every repeat must pass a distinct ++seed. Check this while a sweep is running:
the number of distinct seeds in the ledger should equal the number of runs, and
unique successes should rise rather than plateau.


Running it
----------

Pick two free cards and confirm they are actually idle; cards here turn over on
a scale of hours.

    nvidia-smi --query-gpu=index,uuid,memory.used,temperature.gpu --format=csv,noheader

One target per card, each walking its three algorithms to the budget, with the
seed varied per repeat and a curve point recorded every GPU-hour. The sweep
driver belongs in branching_schedules/scripts/ rather than /tmp, so the run is
reproducible from the repository alone.

Per (target, algorithm) the loop is:

    complexa generate configs/search_binder_local_pipeline.yaml \
        ++run_name=f7_<target>_<algo>_s<seed> \
        ++generation.task_name=<target> \
        ++generation.search.algorithm=<algo> \
        ++seed=<distinct seed>

repeated until the cumulative elapsed time for that pair reaches the budget,
recording elapsed and cumulative seconds per repeat in a ledger.

The evaluate and analyze stages are then run per target exactly as in
table_2_generation.txt, and unique successes are read from the Foldseek cluster
count.

A possible shortcut, with a caveat: in the Table 2 verification run, scRMSD
rejected nothing, so ipAE and pLDDT from the generate stage alone gave the same
success set as the full pipeline. If that holds here it removes the evaluate
stage and cuts roughly 45 % of the compute. It was observed on two easy targets
only, and hard targets are where refolding is most likely to fail, so it must be
checked on the hard target before being relied on.


Environment
-----------

Same as Task 1. Pin one GPU by UUID, and set the JAX and PyTorch memory
variables before any run, because the generator and the AF2 reward share one
card:

    export CUDA_DEVICE_ORDER=PCI_BUS_ID
    export CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=GPU-<uuid>
    export XLA_PYTHON_CLIENT_PREALLOCATE=false
    export XLA_PYTHON_CLIENT_MEM_FRACTION=0.3
    export PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF=expandable_segments:True

Long sweeps go in tmux. /bluesclues-data runs near full and is shared, so check
df -h before starting and be aware a sweep can die on a full disk through no
fault of its own.
